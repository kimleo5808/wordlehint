A row of four green squares and one stubborn gray tells your friends exactly how close you came. A screenshot of that same row gives them four letters of the answer. Here's how to share the near miss while everyone else still has a puzzle to play.

## Send the colored squares to the group chat

Once you've finished the daily Wordle, open the results screen and tap **Share**. Choose your conversation if a share menu appears. If the game says it copied your result, open the chat and paste it into a new message.

The first line gives the puzzle number and your score. Under it, each row of five squares represents one guess. Here's what the grid from a four-guess solve can look like:

Keep the puzzle number if your group plays across time zones. Someone reading your message over breakfast can tell which puzzle you played, even if it's already tomorrow where you live.

## Show a page from your puzzle journal

If you keep your guesses on paper, the interesting part might be the page itself. Leave a five-square margin beside your written guesses and shade one row after each turn. When you're ready to take a photo, lay an opaque card over the words. You'll still see which turn finally broke the puzzle open.

![A hand holds an index card over the guesses in a notebook, leaving three rows of colored squares visible.](/images/blog/share-wordle-results/covered-guesses.webp)

Check the edges of the photo, too. A penciled list of possible endings in the margin can give a friend as much help as seeing your last guess.

## If the chat won't accept your iPhone photo

If the picture you took ends in `.heic` and your group forum asks for a PNG, open [HEICHub’s HEIC to PNG converter](https://heichub.com/). Select the photo, choose **Convert to PNG**, then download the finished image. The conversion runs in your browser. Open the PNG to check the crop and attach that copy to your post.

For a caption, “I stared at row three through half a cup of coffee” tells the story. Save the word you almost played for the friend who replies, “Finished. What was your third guess?”
